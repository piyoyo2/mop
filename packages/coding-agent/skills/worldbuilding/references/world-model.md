# World model

A world is a system. Every element constrains another; nothing in it is only atmosphere.

## The layers, in dependency order

### 1. Rules

What is possible and what it costs. Magic, technology, law, physics — whatever this world runs on.

Every capability needs a limit, and every limit needs someone paying.

> A world where magic is free is a wallpaper. A world where magic costs memory, and someone is keeping a list of whose memories were spent, is a story.

### 2. Geography

Where things are, and what the geography forces on the people in it. A coast, a mountain range, a drought, a trade route — geography is constraints, not scenery.

### 3. Factions

Who wants what, and what stops them. A faction is a position, a resource, and an opponent. Two factions that want the same thing and have no reason to yield are a plot; two factions that want different things are background.

### 4. People

The individuals who carry the factions. Each one wants something that is not the same as their faction's goal.

### 5. History

What already happened that makes the above inevitable. History is what the characters did not choose.

## Drafting order

Rules, then geography, then factions, then people, then history. Each layer constrains the next; a world written in the reverse order has to be retrofitted and shows it.

## The no-slop bar

- **Name things.** A world described entirely in the generic is a setting, not a place.
- **No unearned grandeur.** A city of a million people is a number until something in it is strange, poor, or dangerous in a specific way.
- **No inert factions.** If removing a faction changes nothing, it is decoration.
- **History must bite.** If the history could have gone another way without changing the present, it is not doing work.

## Consistency

Read a drafted world back for contradictions. A world where one thing is impossible and another thing depends on it being possible is two worlds. Resolve it before showing the draft.
