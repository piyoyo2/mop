# The anatomy of a bad card

Six ways a card fails. A card can pass every structural check and still be one of these.

## 1. Genericness

The character could be swapped for any other character with the same role and the card would still work. A courier who is "determined and observant" is a courier in a thousand cards. Specificity is what makes a card worth loading: the exact detail, the odd preference, the thing that only this person would do.

## 2. Scenes that do not earn themselves

A scene is worth writing because of what it reveals, not because it is atmospheric. If a paragraph of scene-setting could be deleted with no change to who the character is or what the user can do, delete it.

## 3. Unedited text

Text that reads like it was assembled rather than written: lists of traits where a paragraph belongs, adjectives stacked three deep, the same fact stated twice in different words, transitions that announce what is about to happen.

## 4. AI tells

Descriptors that could belong to anyone, reactions that everyone has, staging that is always the same, and hedging. The concrete list is in `banned-phrases.md` — read it when you need the exact patterns.

## 5. Structural failures

- The Scenario freezes a moment instead of describing a situation: it must read as present-tense scene verb — sitting, standing, eating, arguing, fighting, walking into, currently at, in.
- The Personality box is an adjective list rather than a set of traits a reader can act against.
- The Opening Message does not hand the user a moment.
- The macros do not resolve.
- The card exceeds its budget and has been cut instead of offloaded.

## 6. Padding

Content added to reach a length rather than to do a job. Padding shows up most in the Scenario and Personality boxes, because those are the ones being made "substantial".

## How to use this

Read the card against each heading. Report what fails, with a location. Do not report a heading that passes — a review that lists six categories and says four are fine is a review that has not read anything.
